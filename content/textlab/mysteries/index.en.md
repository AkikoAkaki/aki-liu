**不思議.**

I wrote these thirty-three inexplicable passages in chronological order. When I finished, I looked back at what I'd written, and honestly they're the kind of draft-like things that should just be thrown in the trash. I doubted whether anyone could actually read through them, since I wrote them without thinking—what maid's bell cleaning a room, what García Márquez. I kind of wanted to delete them right then. But to preserve the sequence of thirty-three, I'd have to write more if I cut anything. Did I need to do that? I kept flipping back, staring at each strangely shaped thing I'd written.

In the end I gave up—I didn't even rearrange the order. What I'd written was really just a little odd inner mapping. Of course I intended for them to be seen, and I desperately wanted them to be seen—but I thought what would be seen was thirty-three versions of me, and the me writing this passage right now is equally incapable of representing all of me. This isn't thirty-three splits of personality. It's thirty-three knots of different shapes tied along the flowing ribbon of time, and through the splicing of texts, I've cinematically released the images of those thirty-three mes. I can rewind and watch what past versions of me looked like; I can stand at a different angle and appreciate, or examine, everything at the moment I wrote those lines—mood, state, relationships, weather, events, what I knew at the time. I know you have some impression of me—any kind of impression. I hope you don't anchor me to the shell of any one of them. What I mean is: I hope that every time you see me appear, it makes you happy.

Finally, thank you to those who made it this far—thank you for watching, for tolerating the thirty-three faces of me. I love you. If you can really see this, please say something, or I won't know who to love.

These thirty-three mes have gradually receded. The next time you meet me, it might be the thirty-fourth.
