A record of contact—thinkers, writers, and artists whose work has shaped how I see and write. Not a canon, not a reading list. Just the residue of encounters that stuck.

---

## Philosophy & Theory

Thinkers in critical theory, continental philosophy, and political thought whose frameworks I return to constantly—Baudrillard and Debord on the image and spectacle; Deleuze and Guattari on desire, affect, and assemblage; Agamben on bare life and the state of exception; Benjamin on ruins, allegory, and historical time; Rancière on the distribution of the sensible; Blanchot on writing and disaster; Foucault on power and the body; Lacan and Derrida on the limits of language; Badiou on truth and the event; Han on burnout and transparency; Cioran on negation and sleeplessness; Žižek on ideology; Azuma and Karatani on postmodernity in East Asia; Yuk Hui on cosmotechnics.

## Fiction

A range held together by formal intensity rather than style: the violent interiority of Mishima, the spectral tenderness of Miyazawa, Pessoa's heteronyms, Kafka's procedural dread, Borges's geometric infinities, Carver's silences, Houellebecq's cold sociology, Tokarczuk's mythic mobility, Aira's improvised delirium, DFW's maximalist grief, Shibusawa's decadent arcana—and the precise, low-register melancholy of contemporary Japanese and Korean women's fiction.

## Poetry

Poets of rupture, grief, and precision. Celan's language pushed to and past its breaking point; Vallejo's viscera on the page; Tranströmer's quiet catastrophes; Baudelaire's fleurs; Plath's controlled combustion; Eliot's montage of ruins; Ungaretti's stripped syllables; René Char's aphoristic resistance; Luo Fu's sinographic density; Yosano Akiko's erotic sovereignty; Le Corbusier's manifestos as prose-poetry; Saigo Tahi's luminous, net-native fragmentation.

- Yosano Akiko (与謝野晶子)
- Tomas Tranströmer
- Giuseppe Ungaretti

## Music

My listening is wide and doesn't settle. Most of it orbits Japan—underground and mainstream pop, jazz, city pop, post-punk, noise rock, and the stranger edges of J-rock. Beyond that: jazz house and hip-hop, instrumental jazz and blues across eras, independent and alternative music from various scenes, and extreme metal and hardcore in most of its subgenres.
